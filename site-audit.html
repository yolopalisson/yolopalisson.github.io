Five phrases that land

  1. Visibility isn't the constraint. Capture is.
  2. CLS isn't a vanity metric. It's a measurable leak in the lead path.
  3. Palisson proves I can build a stable site. It also proves what happens when nobody feeds it.
  4. Cybit already has the engine. My job is to stop the leak and prove the lift.
  5. I'd rather fix the two things costing conversions today than the ten that would be nice next quarter.

If asked what you'd do first

Run PageSpeed on the homepage and the three highest-value campaign pages. Trace the CLS source in the filmstrip. Confirm which element is shifting before touching anything else. Then reserve space for the HubSpot forms and instrument the lead path so the fix can be proven.

Cybit

Leak. CLS 0.9 on both mobile and desktop

Heaviest third-party stack in the comparison: HubSpot, LeadForensics, Demandbase and browser speed tags. Layout shifts on load suppress form completion and webinar registration, the two events the role is hired to move.

Strength. LCP around 1.2s on desktop

Hero content loads fast. Speed is not the bottleneck. Stability is.

Strength. Conversion infrastructure exists

HubSpot forms, campaign pages and webinar CTAs are all in place. The plumbing for qualified enquiries works. It needs a stable front end and proper instrumentation.

NMKCA

Miss. Zero clicks on 500+ high-intent impressions

Ranks positions 1.2 to 3.4 for "chartered accountant johannesburg" and similar, and earns nothing on them. Brand queries convert at 28.7% CTR. The site works when people know the name. Non-brand capture does not.

Leak. CLS 0.9

Identical layout instability to Cybit. WordPress with unmanaged script loading.

Risk. 94% of clicks land on the homepage

No service or location page is doing commercial work. The second-best performing asset is a 2017 company profile PDF, which cannot be tracked, tested or converted.

Miss. No rich results

Zero review or product snippets across six months. For a professional services firm, schema is unclaimed territory.

Palisson

Strength. CLS around zero on both devices

Hand coded, no page builder, explicit dimensions, no dynamic resizing. Proof the candidate can ship a stable layout when controlling the stack.

Unfed. 676 users, roughly 55 qualified

90% direct traffic, with three known data centre cities in the top seven. Real audience aligns with the 9 search clicks plus referral traffic.

Unfed. Impressions fell 80% in three months

135 in July down to 27 in late October. No new content, no internal linking refresh. Even a stable site loses relevance without a growth engine.

Risk. 80.7% bounce on the resume page

People land and leave without clicking anything else. Same pattern as NMKCA's ranking PDF. A high-value page with no onward journey.

Signal. Four visitors from chatgpt.com

Small, but forward-looking. AI assistant referral is a real channel now and worth tracking separately at Cybit.

The first month, week by week

Week 1 — baseline

PageSpeed on the homepage, top three service pages and campaign landing pages, mobile and desktop. Trace the CLS source in the filmstrip.

Week 2 — stabilise

Reserve space for HubSpot forms and banners. Set explicit dimensions on hero media. Audit which third-party tags load before interaction.

Week 3 — instrument

GA4 events on form view, start, submit and CRM sync. Confirm the measurement is accurate before claiming any improvement.

Week 4 — report

Before and after on CLS, form completion rate and webinar registration rate. Agree the next sprint with the CMO.

Core Web Vitals

CWV — Core Web Vitals. Google's three page-experience metrics, used as a ranking signal and a proxy for user experience: LCP, CLS and INP.
LCP — Largest Contentful Paint. How long until the largest visible element renders, usually the hero image or headline. Good is 2.5 seconds or less. Cybit desktop is about 1.2s, so speed is not the problem.
CLS — Cumulative Layout Shift. How much visible content moves unexpectedly while loading. A form that appears late and pushes the call-to-action down scores badly. Good is 0.1 or less. Cybit and NMKCA both sit at 0.9. Palisson at roughly zero.
INP — Interaction to Next Paint. How quickly the page responds to a tap or click. Replaced First Input Delay in 2024. Good is 200 milliseconds or less. Degrades when third-party scripts occupy the main thread.
TBT — Total Blocking Time. Milliseconds the main thread is blocked by long tasks before the page becomes interactive. A lab proxy for INP. Good is 200ms or less. Heavy tag stacks push this up.
TTFB — Time to First Byte. How long the server takes to start responding. Good is 0.8 seconds or less. Points at hosting, caching and CDN rather than front-end code.
FCP — First Contentful Paint. When the first text or image appears. Useful as a floor, less useful as a target on its own.

Search and analytics

GSC — Google Search Console. Google's free tool showing which queries a site appeared for, how often, at what position and how many clicks. Source of the NMKCA and Palisson search data.
GA4 — Google Analytics 4. The current analytics platform. Event-based rather than session-based. Source of the Palisson traffic, engagement and geography data.
GTM — Google Tag Manager. A container that manages marketing and analytics tags without editing site code. Central to tracking QA, and the role's second-biggest gap after PHP.
SERP — Search Engine Results Page. What Google returns for a query, including organic listings, local pack, People Also Ask, images and ads.
CTR — Click-Through Rate. Clicks divided by impressions. NMKCA's zero-click queries are a CTR failure, not a ranking failure.
PAA — People Also Ask. The expandable question block in Google results. A common cause of impressions with no clicks, and one hypothesis for the NMKCA pattern.
Schema / structured data. Machine-readable markup, usually JSON-LD, that tells search engines what a page is about. Enables rich results and improves visibility in AI-generated answers.

Commercial

CRO — Conversion Rate Optimisation. Structured testing and iteration to increase the share of visitors who complete a desired action. Heatmaps, funnel analysis, A/B tests.
MQL — Marketing Qualified Lead. A contact judged ready for sales follow-up based on behaviour or fit. The bridge between a webinar registration and pipeline.
CRM — Customer Relationship Management. The system holding contact and deal records. HubSpot at Cybit, Salesforce in the candidate's background.
A/B test. Splitting traffic between two versions of a page to determine which performs better. Only valid when volume is sufficient, which is why cluster-level metrics beat single-query tests.

Technical

DOM — Document Object Model. The browser's live tree of page elements. Heavy page builders inflate it, which slows rendering and interaction.
CDN — Content Delivery Network. A geographically distributed caching layer. Cloudflare in Cybit's case.
CI — Continuous Integration. Automated checks that run on every code change. Lighthouse CI makes Core Web Vitals a release gate rather than a monthly report.
PHP. The server-side language WordPress runs on. Required in the job description. The biggest technical gap to close.

Things to avoid saying

  • Don't claim PHP or GTM depth. Frame both as proof-of-concept work in progress, and keep the conversation on diagnosis and prioritisation.
  • Don't present Palisson's 676 users as a win. Present it as a diagnostic. The gap between 676 and 9 is the point.
  • Don't lead with Cybit. Lead with what you built, then what you found.
  • Don't quote a mobile decimal you can't back with a screenshot. Lab runs vary between audits.
